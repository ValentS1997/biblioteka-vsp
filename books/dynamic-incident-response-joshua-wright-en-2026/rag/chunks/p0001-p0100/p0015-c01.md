What follows is a practical, iterative incident response framework built on real-world experience. Like this
preface, an effective response starts wherever the situation demands, not where a checklist says it should.

WHAT IS IN THIS BOOK?
We wrote this book to provide a comprehensive guide for responding to cybersecurity incidents. It covers
the essential concepts, methodologies, and tools needed to effectively manage and mitigate the impact of
breaches and attacks in modern organizations. The book is structured to guide readers through the entire
incident response lifecycle, applying practical techniques learned through real-world scenarios.

The book is organized into three parts:

Part 1: Elements of Incident Response
  • Chapter 1, Case Study: Supply Chain Calamity opens the book with a fictional scenario following a cloud
    solutions architect as a supply chain compromise unfolds, grounding the concepts that follow in a
    realistic incident narrative.
  • Chapter 2, Introduction explains why the choices responders make under pressure shape incident
   outcomes, and examines the changing demands on modern incident response teams.
  • Chapter 3, A Short History of Incident Response traces the early cybersecurity incidents that prompted
    the development of formal response models, then walks through the foundational frameworks from
   CERT and CIAC through the SANS step-by-step approach and NIST SP 800-61.
  • Chapter 4, Getting Started establishes foundational terminology and concepts for incident response that
    we’ll use throughout the book.
  • Chapter 5, Incident Response Models and Their Limitations reviews the PICERL model, NIST SP 800-61,
   and variations of each, then identifies limitations that make them inadequate for modern incident
    response.

Part 2: A Dynamic Approach to Incident Response
  • Chapter 6, A Dynamic Approach to Incident Response introduces the Dynamic Approach to Incident
   Response (DAIR) model, explaining  its waypoints, outcomes, and the response actions loop that
    distinguishes it from traditional linear models.
  • Chapter 7, Prepare Activity covers organizational and team readiness, including policy development,
   playbook  creation, tabletop  exercises, security monitoring, and the integration  of cyber threat
    intelligence.
  • Chapter 8, Detect Activity explores detection methodologies including signature-based, behavioral, and
    AI-assisted approaches, along with the technical, human, and cloud-native data sources that feed
    detection efforts.
  • Chapter 9, Verify and Triage Activities addresses how to validate events of interest, enrich indicators
   through pivoting and cyber threat intelligence, perform initial risk classification, and present findings to
    decision makers.
  • Chapter 10, Response Actions Loop describes the iterative cycle of scope, contain, eradicate, and recover
    activities, including iteration triggers and criteria for determining when to exit the loop.
  • Chapter  11, Scope Activity examines methodologies for determining the breadth of compromise,
    including progressive scoping, timeline reconstruction, indicator-based hunting, and techniques for
    addressing visibility gaps.
