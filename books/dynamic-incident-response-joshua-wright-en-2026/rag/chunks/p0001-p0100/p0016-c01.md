  • Chapter 13, Eradicate Activity addresses removing attacker persistence, conducting root-cause analysis,

Part 3: Special Considerations in Incident Response
  • Chapter 16, Accelerating Incident Response with AI explores practical applications of generative AI in
    incident response, from prompt engineering and log analysis through Model Context Protocol (MCP)
    integration and agentic workflows, with guidance on verification, trust, and organizational policy.
  • Chapter 17, Incident Response for Ransomware applies the DAIR model to ransomware and multi-vector
    extortion  campaigns,  covering  ransomware-specific  preparation,  verification  indicators,  data
    exfiltration detection, decryption assessment, backup strategies, and post-incident debrief.
  • Chapter 18, Incident Response for Cloud Systems addresses the distinct challenges of incident response in
   cloud environments, including identity-first containment, ephemeral resource investigation, and cloud-
    native recovery techniques.
  • Chapter 19, Incident Response for Operational Technology examines incident response in operational
   technology environments where  safety,  physical processes, and  specialized protocols introduce
    constraints not present in traditional IT incidents.
  • Chapter 20, Integrating DAIR with NIST CSF 2.0 demonstrates how DAIR activities map to NIST CSF 2.0
    functions, showing how compliance artifacts emerge naturally from effective response operations and
   how to satisfy audit requirements through documented DAIR activities.

Each chapter concludes with Step-by-Step sections that translate concepts into actionable checklists
readers can apply immediately.

WHO IS THIS BOOK FOR?
This book is intended to serve as a resource for cybersecurity professionals working in incident response
roles, including incident responders, security analysts, IT professionals, and anyone interested in learning
about effective incident management. While the book addresses advanced topics, its content is accessible to
readers with varying levels of cybersecurity and incident response experience. Readers should have a basic
understanding of networking, operating systems, and cybersecurity concepts to fully benefit from this
book’s material.

This book is not intended to be a comprehensive guide to all aspects of cybersecurity or incident response
investigation. Instead,  it focuses on practical techniques and methodologies applicable to real-world
scenarios. Readers who influence their organization’s incident response practices will likely find the most
value in this book.
