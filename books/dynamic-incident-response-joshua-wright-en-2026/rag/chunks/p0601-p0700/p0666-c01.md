COVER PHOTOGRAPH
I took the cover photograph during a trip to Arches National Park in Utah. The formation on the cover is
Double Arch, one of the park’s sandstone arches. I captured the original image on an iPhone 15 Pro Max,
then processed it in my darkroom, printing a transparency and using a print enlarger to produce a wet plate
collodion print.

I chose this image and this process for a few reasons:

  • The wet plate collodion process is a historical photographic technique that produces one-of-a-kind
    images. The artifacts visible on the cover (the dark vignetting, the streaks along the edges, the dust
   caught in the emulsion) are evidence of an imperfect, unpredictable process. As an artist, you learn to
    let go of control and embrace what the chemistry gives you. That felt like a fitting metaphor for incident
    response, where responders rarely control the situation and instead work with what they find.
  • The path from modern digital capture to a 19th-century printing technique mirrors the book itself,
   which draws on foundational ideas from decades of incident response practice and applies them to
    current threats and environments.
  • There is a tension in the image between the massive surrounding rock and the thin span of the arch
    overhead. The formation looks solid from a distance, but up close, the stone is thinner and more
   weathered than it appears from afar. That quality resonates with the work described in this book, in
   which the structures organizations build to protect themselves are continually tested by forces beyond
    their control.

I hope the cover sets a tone for what you find inside: work that is careful, imperfect, and shaped by what
you find along the way.
