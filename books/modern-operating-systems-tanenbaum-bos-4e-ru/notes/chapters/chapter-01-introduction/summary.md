# Chapter-end summary

Provenance: PDF pages 106–107; chunks `p0106:c01` and `p0107:c01`.

The chapter concludes with the following source-grounded points:

- An operating system can be viewed both as a **resource manager** and as an **extended machine** that presents more convenient abstractions than raw hardware.
- The historical path runs from systems that largely replaced a human operator through batch processing and multiprogramming to modern multitasking systems and personal/mobile platforms.
- Understanding operating systems requires basic hardware context: processors, memory and I/O devices connected through buses.
- Core OS concepts introduced here are processes, memory/address-space management, I/O, filesystems and security.
- A system-call set expresses the services the OS actually provides to programs. The UNIX examples are grouped around process management, files, directories/filesystems and miscellaneous operations.
- Operating systems may be structured as monolithic systems, layered systems, microkernels, client-server systems, virtual machines or exokernels.

The chapter is intentionally a high-level map. The detailed mechanisms are delegated to the later chapters on processes/threads, memory, filesystems, I/O, deadlocks, virtualization, multiprocessors and security.
