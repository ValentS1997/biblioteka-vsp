# Chapter 13 summary

Provenance: synthesized from PDF pages 1110–1119. The source contains no separate chapter-end summary.

- The chapter's purpose is to help readers continue studying operating systems after the main 12 teaching chapters.
- Section 13.1 organizes recommended books and papers by subject area rather than presenting new operating-system mechanisms.
- The recommendation categories mirror the book's main topics: general operating systems, processes/threads, memory, filesystems, I/O, deadlock, virtualization/cloud, multiprocessors, security, Linux/Android, Windows and OS design.
- The source explicitly points to major research venues and publications: SOSP, OSDI, EuroSys, ACM Transactions on Computer Systems and ACM SIGOPS Operating Systems Review.
- Several recommendations are broad textbooks; others are standards, implementation-focused books, classic papers, surveys or design essays.
- The design-reading section closes with works by Saltzer & Kaashoek, Brooks, Cooke et al., Corbató, Crowley, Lampson and Wirth, reinforcing the book's recurring themes of simplicity, system design experience and avoiding unnecessary complexity.
- Section 13.2 does not reproduce the promised detailed alphabetical bibliography in the local PDF; it says that the list can be downloaded from the publisher's site.
