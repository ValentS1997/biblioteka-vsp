# Chapter-end summary

Provenance: PDF page 1106; chunk `p1106:c01`.

The chapter's explicit conclusions are:

- Operating-system design begins with defining the system's goals.
- Interfaces should be simple, complete and efficiently implementable.
- A system should present clear paradigms for user interaction, execution and data.
- The implementation should be well structured; layering and client–server/microkernel architecture are among the available approaches.
- Internal components should be orthogonal, and policy should be separated clearly from mechanism.
- Naming, binding time, static versus dynamic structures and implementation order materially affect system structure.
- Performance matters, but optimization should not destroy system structure.
- Useful performance techniques include time/space tradeoffs, caching, hints, locality and optimization of the common case.
- Large-system construction differs fundamentally from development by a team of two or three people. Team organization and project management become major determinants of success.
- Operating systems evolve in response to new environments such as hypervisors/cloud systems, multicore hardware, large address spaces, mobile/battery-powered systems and embedded systems.
